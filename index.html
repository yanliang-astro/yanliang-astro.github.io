Yan Liang

Astrophysics & AI

I am a 51 Pegasi b Fellow at Yale University, where I work with Malena Rice and develop AI methods for scientific discovery in astronomy. My work provides powerful tools for studying a variety of astrophysical objects—from distant galaxies (SPENDER I, II, DESI outliers) to exoplanets orbiting stars in our solar neighborhood (AESTRA I, II).

My most recent work focused on developing generative AI models to disentangle stellar activity from planetary signals in radial-velocity data, with the goal of uncovering small, Earth-like planets. My long-term vision is to build a foundation model for stellar spectroscopy that learns shared patterns of stellar variability and instrumental effects across many stars and spectrographs. By transferring this knowledge to new targets, the model could adapt with fewer spectra per star.

I received my Ph.D. in Astrophysical Sciences from Princeton University in 2025, advised by Joshua Winn and Peter Melchior, and my B.A. in Physics from UC Berkeley.
